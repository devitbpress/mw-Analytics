## Introduction

Community-based coastal eco-tourism requires clear, accessible spatial information to guide visitors while protecting sensitive conservation zones.

## Problem setting

Rural tourism destinations often lack centralized spatial platforms, resulting in fragmented information and uneven tourist distribution across local heritage assets.

## Data

GPS field tracks, point-of-interest (POI) spatial coordinates, community asset inventories, and drone basemaps.

## Method

Data was structured into GeoJSON vector layers and deployed via an interactive client-side web mapping interface (React + Leaflet), integrated with local service listings.

## Validation

User testing with 30 trial visitors demonstrated an 85% task completion rate for destination route planning (illustrative).

## Limitations

Low cellular bandwidth in remote coastal areas requires lightweight vector tile optimization and offline caching strategies.

## Conclusion

Interactive spatial atlases empower local communities to manage eco-tourism assets while improving visitor navigation.
